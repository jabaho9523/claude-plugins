# Invention Disclosure Form

Copy this file, rename to `IDF-YYYY-NNN-short-title.md`, fill it in, send to the IP owner.

Write it for a smart colleague who does not work on your project. If a section does not apply, write "not applicable" and why. Do not leave blanks, because a blank reads as an oversight to both a patent examiner and a tax caseworker.

## B. The invention

**B1. What problem does this solve?**
Two to four sentences. State it as a technical problem, not a commercial one.

**B2. How was this problem solved before, and why was that inadequate?**
List the approaches you know of, including published methods, commercial tools and Cmbio's own previous approach. Name papers, tools and versions.

**B3. What is the invention?**
Describe it so someone skilled in the field could reproduce it. Include the steps, the order, the inputs and the outputs.

**B4. What is the essential part?**
If you had to strip this down to the one thing that makes it work and that nobody else is doing, what is it? This is the part the firm will try to claim.

**B5. What are the variations?**
Other ways to achieve the same effect, other parameter ranges, other organisms or sample types, other deployment contexts. Breadth in the disclosure becomes breadth in the claims.

**B6. What evidence do you have that it works?**
Datasets, benchmarks, comparison against the prior approach, effect sizes. Point to the files.

## C. Documentation of innovation (tax)

This section exists for Skattestyrelsen. It follows the Frascati criteria: novelty, a creative element, and technical or scientific uncertainty. Write it in the past tense, describing the state of knowledge before the work started.

**C1. What was technically or scientifically uncertain when the work began?**
The key question. Not "we had not built it yet" but "it was not known whether X could be achieved, because Y". If there was no genuine uncertainty, say so, and this project probably does not qualify.

**C2. What was the state of the art before this work?**
What existed publicly, and what the gap was.

**C3. What made the approach creative or non-routine?**
Distinguish this from competent application of known methods.

**C4. What was tried and did not work?**
Failed attempts are among the strongest evidence of genuine uncertainty. Do not omit them.

## E. Ownership

**E1. Which client or partner agreements touched this work?**
Name the sponsor, contract reference and date.

**E2. Under those agreements, who owns results arising from the work?**
Foreground versus background. If unsure, write "unsure" and the IP owner will run a clause review.

**E3. Did anyone outside Cmbio contribute to the conception?**
Academic collaborators, contractors, students, advisors. Name them and state whether an assignment exists.

**E4. Was any third-party material used?**
Licensed datasets, open source components and their licences, reference databases with usage restrictions.

## F. Strategy input from the inventor

**F1. If a competitor used this, could Cmbio tell from the outside?**
Consider: would it show in their published methods, their service descriptions, their output formats, their results? Answer high, medium or low, with a reason.

**F2. What does Cmbio sell, or plan to sell, that this sits inside?**

**F3. How long will this stay relevant?**
A patent takes years to grant and lasts twenty. If the technique is obsolete in three, say so.

**F4. Your recommended route, and why.**
Patent, trade secret, defensive publication or no action.
